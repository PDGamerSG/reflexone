# Research basis and evidence for ReflexOne

ReflexOne is an engineering application of established supervised language-understanding methods to a Jev-inspired interface. Its contribution is a reproducible local implementation, annotation/data audits, comparisons, calibrated output experiments and explicit failure analysis. It does not claim a new foundation-model architecture, a reproduction of TypeSafe's training, or a new state of the art.

Primary sources were inspected on **2026-11-04**. This is a bounded investigation of methods and datasets relevant to this project, not a systematic literature review. Source statements, implementation choices and measured results are distinguished below.

## The motivating interface

[Diogo Almeida, *Introducing System One Models & Jev*, TypeSafe, September 15, 2026](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) describes unstructured state going into a model and typed probabilistic decisions coming out, with outputs calculated in parallel rather than autoregressively generated strings. The article names a proprietary architecture, parallel sampler and Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions. Those named components are not described in sufficient detail here to reproduce them.

The transferable idea is an interface suited to ordinary program decisions. ReflexOne implements it through fixed supervised label heads and an extractive decoder, with Pydantic validating the returned values. Its labels and training losses are public and inspectable. The article's speed, pricing and intelligence comparisons are vendor-reported results under different workloads; they are not evidence that ReflexOne matches Jev or has a particular speedup against it. A schema constrains legal values, while semantic predictions can still be false.

It is useful reading, but ReflexOne does not implement a Siamese training objective. The model card is the more direct source for the actual upstream checkpoint used.

## What the local experiments establish

The evidence for this repository's performance is the code, frozen manifests, saved artifacts and reports, rather than the papers above. Main measurements are recorded in [support.json](../reports/support.json), [commands.json](../reports/commands.json), [spam.json](../reports/spam.json), and [support-benchmark.json](../reports/support-benchmark.json). The fixed primary support run remains seed 42. The [three-seed summary](../reports/support-seeds.json), [seed-7 report](../reports/support-seed7.json) and [seed-123 report](../reports/support-seed123.json) expose training variability without choosing a new primary model from test scores.

| Local claim | Evidence | Scope and unresolved issue |
|---|---|---|
| Neural support outperforms the lexical intent baseline in the fixed primary run | Intent micro-F1 0.86862 versus 0.76175, same frozen holdout, separately validation-tuned thresholds | Observed +10.69 percentage points. Several architectural/training factors differ together. A specific contribution from the domain embedding, class weights or fine-tuning is not isolated by this comparison. |
| Training randomness affects the result | Intent micro-F1 0.84885–0.86862 and slot F1 0.59574–0.63511 across seeds 42, 7 and 123 | Same configuration and fixed split; each checkpoint selected on validation. Three runs measure training sensitivity, not independent test populations, a confidence interval or a controlled architectural ablation. |
| Exact semantic extraction is substantially weaker than intent F1 | Exact span precision 0.51815, recall 0.75481, F1 0.61448; complete-frame accuracy 0.46689 | 146 extra predicted spans and 51 missed gold spans show why fine-grained automation remains limited. No slot baseline or normalized-date evaluation was trained. |
| Global temperature modestly improves the primary support test probability scores | NLL 0.03748→0.03671, pooled ECE 0.00876→0.00659; unchanged selected labels | Pooled negative pairs can dominate; two labels lack positive calibration examples. The baseline's Brier score worsens after its calibration, so improvements are not universal. |
| A command rejection policy has held-out OOS evidence | OOS recall 0.923, accepted accuracy 0.96053 at coverage 0.63587 | Calibrated class probabilities remain conditional on supported classes. Only CLINC's supplied OOS population is evaluated. |
| A compact lexical spam model is competitive on the chosen public holdout | Accuracy 0.98643, spam F1 0.94574, precision 0.93846, recall 0.95313 | Test has only 64 spam examples; no current-distribution or temporal generalization claim. |
| Support predictions are inexpensive locally | Warm CPU p50 5.34 ms/p95 6.71 ms, 120 timed cases across six short texts, tokenization/decoding/schema validation included | No process startup, model load, HTTP or concurrent service load. Training uses MPS, while this benchmark uses CPU. No measured comparison with Jev or a remote LLM. |

Support's independent token streams, ontology masks, chosen architecture, class weighting and empirical review cutoff are **implementation choices**. Their mechanism can be explained, but their individual benefit has not been proven by a controlled ablation. Fixed schema membership and exact slice checks are **program invariants**, not empirical semantic-quality claims.

## Inspection log and practical limits

The investigation used direct retrieval of known primary sources and official repositories rather than a broad search-engine survey. On 2026-11-04, the Jev article, NLU++ paper metadata/abstract and source README, MiniLM paper abstract and pinned model card, joint intent/slot paper abstract, calibration paper page, uncertainty-under-shift paper abstract, and AdamW paper abstract were opened. Official CLINC README/license/data and current UCI page/download were inspected. The source locations supporting the decisions are listed in the table above; reading an abstract or official model card is not presented as a full-paper replication.

The data were also read programmatically: all NLU++ folds were audited by the project's preparation pipeline, and both classical profiles were trained on the downloaded public examples. Saved source revisions and content hashes make that acquisition repeatable. Retrieval failures during the initial broader exploration did not become evidence for any chosen method or novelty claim.

The scope leaves several empirical questions open: broader seed coverage and uncertainty intervals; a frozen-encoder ablation; domain-embedding/class-weight ablations; alternative span decoding; more robust rare-label calibration; and modern or shifted external evaluation. Any later experiment must state whether it is validation exploration or a fresh final test. Once the delivered test results inform a model change, that same holdout cannot supply an untouched confirmatory claim.

Read [learning-guide.md](learning-guide.md) for a worked path through the implementation, and [interview-guide.md](interview-guide.md) for ways to explain the work without overstating its novelty or reliability.
